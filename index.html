Stage I: Guiding Question (~5 Minutes)

Central Inquiry Question: "Before psychology became an official science in 1879, how did humans explain emotions, thoughts, and choices?"

Exploratory Class Discussion

  • Myths & Religion: Ancient civilizations often attributed mental states to spirits or divine forces.
  • Pure Philosophy: Thinkers relied on logic and internal reflection rather than controlled experiments.
  • Why the Shift? Why was pure reflection insufficient for understanding how humans actually behave?

Stage II: Philosophical Antecedents & Wundt's Lab (~30 Minutes)

15 min Philosophical Thinkers (Descartes vs. Locke) | 15 min Wundt & Leipzig Lab (1879)

Rationalism 1596 - 1650

René Descartes: Dualism & Reason

  • Core Concept: Interactive Dualism (Mind and body are separate entities that interact via the pineal gland).
  • Rationalism: Knowledge is gained through reason and innate ideas rather than sensory experience alone.
  • Famous Quote: "Cogito, ergo sum" (I think, therefore I am).
Empiricism 1632 - 1704

John Locke: The Blank Slate

  • Core Concept: Tabula Rasa (The mind at birth is a blank slate).
  • Empiricism: All knowledge originates from sensory experience and observation of the environment.
  • Marketing Link: Consumer habits and brand preferences are learned through environmental exposure.
Birth of Psychology as a Science

Wilhelm Wundt & The Leipzig Laboratory (1879)

Leipzig, Germany
1. First Experimental Lab: Established the very first psychology laboratory in 1879, separating psychology from philosophy and biology.
2. Experimental Introspection: Trained subjects to observe and record their conscious experiences under strictly controlled conditions.
3. Structuralism Focus: Aimed to break down mental processes into basic elements (sensations, feelings, perceptions).
Check Your Understanding:
Which philosopher claimed the mind is a "Tabula Rasa" (blank slate)?
John Locke (Empiricism)
What significant event occurred in Leipzig, Germany in 1879?
Wilhelm Wundt founded the first official experimental psychology laboratory.

Stage III: Collaborative Timeline Matrix (~15 Minutes)

Teams of 4 build a chronological roadmap connecting philosophical antecedents to scientific psychology.

Historical Progression Matrix

Era / Date Key Figure & Movement Methodology Used Contribution to Modern Science
Ancient Era Socrates & Plato (Rationalism) Dialectic introspection & logic Pioneered questioning innate human knowledge.
17th Century René Descartes (Dualism) Deductive reasoning Distinguished mental processes from physical body actions.
Late 17th Century John Locke (Empiricism) Sensory observation & experience Laid the foundation for empirical data collection.
1879 Wilhelm Wundt (Scientific Psychology) Controlled laboratory experimentation & Introspection Established psychology as an independent, measurement-based science.

Stage IV: Market Relevance & Synthesis (~10 Minutes)

5 min Connecting Research History to Consumer Understanding + 5 min Exit Ticket

How History Shapes Modern Consumer Research

Why does a marketer care about Wundt's 1879 laboratory?

Locke's Empiricism → Market Research

Marketers don't guess what customers want; they collect empirical data through surveys, focus groups, and website analytics.

Wundt's Lab → Neuromarketing & AB Testing

Controlled lab experiments led directly to tracking eye movements, measuring reaction times, and running controlled A/B ad tests.

5-Minute Exit Ticket Prompt (Individual Submission)

Write 1 sentence: "How did Wilhelm Wundt's work in 1879 transform human behavior from a subject of philosophical debate into something measurable for modern science and marketing?"
